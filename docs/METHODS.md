# Methods implementation

NicheBridge 0.0.1 provides count-constrained deconvolution and a generative trajectory model. `NicheBridge(mode="pair")` selects two-input deconvolution; `mode="joint"` selects joint reference/spatial/bulk deconvolution; `mode="trajectory"` selects diffusion transport. These estimators have different likelihoods and output scales. A count-decoder expectation is not a reverse diffusion reconstruction.

## Mapping to manuscript Methods

| Component | Implementation | Input or output convention |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Spatial expression enhancement | `pp.spatial_enhance` uses coordinate neighbors within slices. | Explicit optional convex smoothing; the original count layer is preserved. |
| ATAC gene activities | `pp.gene_activity` aggregates a supplied weighted peak-to-gene map. | Genome-specific annotation is supplied by the caller. |

| Reverse generation, Eq. 4 | A differentiable probability-flow ODE is integrated with Heun steps. | `reconstruct` returns a selected-feature molecular profile; optional trajectory output includes intermediate states. |

Evaluation metrics and downstream biological analyses are outside the installable modeling package. The six experiments and kidney tutorial contain their own analysis helpers. R analyses are not included.

## Numerical and preprocessing choices


Arrays in the manuscript are features by observations. Python matrices are observations by features. `transport(target, source)` always returns target observations in rows and source observations in columns, with nonnegative entries whose rows sum to one. Reconstruction in the opposite direction uses the transposed relation normalized across its new rows; a zero-mass row caused by numerical underflow receives a uniform fallback. This makes the two projection directions explicit rather than assuming that one rectangular matrix is normalized in both directions. The trajectory implementation uses symmetric reconstruction mean-square errors in both directions, whereas Eqs. 7 and 9 write a directed squared Frobenius loss. Symmetrization and division by the number of entries are explicit implementation choices; their weights must be calibrated for a full experiment.

The generative engine selects the Brownian special case `f=0`, `g=sigma`, `T=1`. Its conditioned marginal has mean `(1-t)*x0 + t*xT` and variance `sigma**2*t*(1-t)`. Conditional score matching uses an x0-parameterized score and variance weighting. The probability-flow drift is algebraically equivalent to the Doob term minus half the score term. Numerical integration uses a finite endpoint cutoff and Heun steps on a logit-spaced time grid. These choices implement the general Methods formulation and are not a claim that the original unpublished training used this exact diffusion schedule.

Analytic forward bridge samples are used only for the score-matching objective. During joint training, all branches also start from their learned endpoints and advance through a differentiable reverse probability-flow rollout. At every Heun step, source intermediate states are projected into the shared latent space and transported to the corresponding score-network conditioning channels. The same learned relation is held fixed throughout that rollout. Molecular transport penalties are evaluated at a shared random subset of grid times; their gradients pass through the numerical solver into the score networks, endpoint decoders, encoders and learned relations. There is no substitution of forward bridge samples or decoder predictions for these reverse-state penalties.

`coupling_steps` controls the training rollout and defaults to 16; `trajectory_times` controls the number of its sampled evaluation times and defaults to 2. Sampling is uniform over noninitial points of the logit-spaced grid, so it is not uniform over continuous diffusion time. Selected state losses are averaged. `ode_steps` controls inference separately and defaults to 64. The finite cutoff defaults to 0.001 at both ends. Larger training/inference step counts trade computation for numerical resolution and should be checked for convergence in a substantive analysis. The score-matching times are sampled continuously and uniformly between the cutoffs.

`reconstruct` in the full model performs an ODE solve with the observation's own latent state, endpoint and slice condition, and a zero cross-dataset conditioning channel. This path is trained during independent pretraining and, for coupled datasets, by the default 10% condition-dropout score matching. For a single spatial branch with multiple slices, the reverse training and reconstruction paths have no cross-dataset channel. For cross-dataset models, the joint coupling objective optimizes cross-conditioned paths while `reconstruct` returns the marginal reconstruction path. Configuring both `pretrain_epochs=0` and `condition_dropout=0` removes intentional training of that zero-context path and is unsuitable when marginal reconstruction is required. With `use_diffusion=True`, `generate(source, target)` explicitly conditions the target ODE on the source's transported reverse states and never falls back to a decoder-only output. Generation is transductive: its relation and output locations are those fitted by the model, not an evaluated prediction on unseen target observations.

The endpoint is a learned decoder output. The objective also includes configurable endpoint mean-square error and Gaussian-prior KL regularization to stabilize fitting. Cross-slice distribution alignment uses symmetric KL to the moment-matched Gaussian posterior transported from the other slice. This is an explicit conditional-VAE estimator for the manuscript's schematic alignment term. It is recorded separately from trajectory-state consistency in the training history.

The main manuscript specifies 3,000 HVGs and log1p but does not specify an HVG flavor or a full preprocessing recipe. The count-constrained models retain raw counts for their count likelihood. Their `pp.prepare(..., gene_selection="hvg", n_genes=3000)` route uses Scanpy Seurat normalized dispersion. The trajectory engine defaults to `hvg_method="scanpy"`, uses Seurat normalized dispersion on pooled log1p rows in the common-gene space and selects at most 3,000 features with deterministic ranking. Independent RNA/ATAC feature spaces have separate panels. If fewer features are available, all are retained. Degenerate Scanpy selection raises an actionable error; `hvg_method="variance"` is an explicit alternative rather than a silent fallback. Selected feature names and preprocessing choices are recorded. Continuous bulk expression and precomputed ATAC activities must be supplied on their nonnegative, unlogged scale; they must not be treated as raw integer counts. Library normalization is configurable. Passing already logged X would apply log1p twice and is not a supported input convention.

The main manuscript refers to supplementary spatial expression enhancement without specifying its kernel. `pp.spatial_enhance` implements an optional, configurable convex combination with within-slice spatial neighbors. This operational choice is visible to the caller and preserves the original count layer. It should not be described as an exact implementation of an unavailable supplementary formula.

Niche assembly optionally z-scores numeric fields and divides each block by the square root of its width. Domains are one-hot encoded. This optional scaling prevents reconstructed gene count from dominating solely through dimensionality; `standardize=False` retains the supplied numeric fields. The latent representation defines the returned spot order when present.

## Controlled model variants

The full trajectory model defaults to `use_vae=True` and `use_diffusion=True`. `use_vae=False` removes the posterior variance head, stochastic latent sampling, prior KL and slice posterior KL. It retains reverse diffusion and trajectory-state alignment.

`use_diffusion=False` removes the score network, bridge sampling, score matching and reverse ODE integration. The variational encoder, its prior KL and slice posterior KL remain active when `use_vae=True`. Training uses endpoint reconstruction and latent correspondence losses; slice latent MSE replaces reverse-state consistency. Inference decodes the posterior mean. This output is identified as endpoint reconstruction and is not described as reverse diffusion. Trajectory output cannot be requested in this mode.

The kidney `no_batch_removal` variant omits slice conditioning and cross-slice relations. The RNA and gene-score ablations fit only the retained molecular branch. These controls remove named mechanisms without using biological evaluation labels as training targets.

## Computational scope and data separation


The generative transport engine currently trains full-batch relations. The default limits are 20 million entries per dense molecular matrix, 4 million entries per relation, and 25 million stored trajectory entries. Coupled training also checks the sum of molecular entries times `coupling_steps + 1` against the trajectory limit. This is an explicit size guard, not an exact GPU/RAM usage estimate: backpropagation stores network activations in addition to molecular states. Normal reconstruction avoids retaining a full trajectory unless requested. Conditional generation and requested trajectory histories are also checked against the trajectory limit.

Large datasets require an explicit representative subset or a future minibatch/approximate-transport implementation. The inspected kidney dataset has 31,745 spots, 3,000 genes and eight sections. Its molecular matrix alone contains 95,235,000 entries, and all pairwise section relations total approximately 439 million entries before gradients and activations. It therefore cannot be described as a complete fresh fit under the default full-batch limits. A tutorial subset of 32 spots per section and 256 genes contains 256 spots and is appropriate for an execution demonstration, not for recovering the complete kidney landscape or reproducing reported biological results. Increasing limits without addressing these costs is not a scalable training solution.

Learned unpaired cross-modal correspondences require biological validation; a nonnegative matrix and a finite training loss do not establish correspondence accuracy.

Phenotype, cell-type evaluation composition, anatomical annotations and marker benchmark labels must remain outside generative fitting. The trajectory model reads expression, observation/feature IDs, and explicit slice labels. Clinical and cell identity fields are projected only after fitting. Reference-guided deconvolution intentionally uses reference cell labels; spatial evaluation labels are never fitting targets.

## Consistency boundaries


| Component | What matches the Methods structure | Explicit implementation choice or remaining boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data-specific bridges and score matching | Independent encoders, learned endpoints and conditional scores; analytic bridge sampling and score-matching targets. | Brownian diffusion, x0 score parameterization, a Gaussian latent posterior, and additional latent/slice/context inputs operationalize the general equations. |
| Reverse trajectories and coupling | Actual probability-flow ODE states are coupled across common times; transport penalties directly train score parameters through reverse integration. | Finite Heun rollouts, logit time grid, sampled grid losses and a finite endpoint cutoff approximate the continuous formulation. |
| Cross-scale and cross-modal losses | Common-gene molecular constraints or shared-latent modality constraints, plus latent correspondence, use one learned relation across times. | The manuscript writes directed squared Frobenius losses; the implementation uses symmetric, size-normalized MSE and row-normalizes the reverse relation. These are materially different loss scalings and must be reported. |
| Slice alignment | A shared slice-conditioned spatial branch, reverse-state coupling and conditional Gaussian posterior alignment are implemented. | Softmax-normalized latent correspondences and symmetric KL to a moment-matched transported posterior are explicit estimators of the manuscript's schematic CVAE term, not a recovered original implementation. |
| Complete objective | Bridge, scale, modal and slice components activate according to available inputs. | Endpoint reconstruction and Gaussian-prior KL are additional configurable regularizers; the default objective is not term-for-term identical to Eq. 12. |
| Spatial maps and molecular outputs | ID-aligned matrix multiplication maps phenotypes and cell identities; reverse ODEs produce molecular landscapes and conditional generations. | Reconstruction uses the selected feature panel on log1p scale, with default library normalization and final nonnegative projection. It is neither raw counts nor an automatic replacement for an archived layer of unknown scale. |
| Preprocessing and spatial information | Shared HVGs, log1p, optional coordinate enhancement and supplied peak-to-gene aggregation are implemented. | HVG flavor, library normalization, enhancement kernel and gene-activity annotation must be specified. The missing supplementary kernel cannot be verified from the main document. |
| Kidney execution | All eight sections can participate in one shared spatial branch using an explicitly sampled subset. | Full 31,745-spot fitting, full-cohort biological validity and manuscript figure/performance reproduction have not been established. |

The supported conclusion is that the core mechanisms in the Methods have executable implementations, with the differences and numerical choices above. It is not a claim of exact equivalence to the manuscript's equations under their original, unspecified settings, or to the original experiment implementation.
